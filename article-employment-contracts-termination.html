Employment Contracts & Unfair Termination in Kenya

7 min read May 22, 2026
Reader note: This article provides general information, not legal advice for a specific matter. Laws and procedures can change; confirm current requirements and seek advice on your circumstances. Please verify the cited legislation and decisions before relying on them.

Employment relationships in Kenya are governed by a complex legal framework that balances employer rights with employee protections. Understanding the requirements for valid employment contracts and the legal standards for termination is essential for both employers and employees to avoid costly disputes.

1. Essential Elements of Valid Employment Contracts

Under the Employment Act, every employment contract must include:

  • Parties Information: Full names and addresses of employer and employee
  • Job Description: Clear description of duties and responsibilities
  • Remuneration Details: Salary, payment frequency, and benefits structure
  • Working Hours: Normal working hours, overtime provisions, and rest periods
  • Leave Entitlements: Annual leave, sick leave, maternity/paternity leave
  • Termination Conditions: Notice periods and grounds for termination
  • Disciplinary Procedures: Grievance handling and disciplinary processes

2. Types of Employment Contracts in Kenya

Contract Type Duration Legal Requirements
Permanent Contract Indefinite Full Employment Act protections, notice period required for termination
Fixed-Term Contract Specific period Automatic termination at expiry, renewable with consent
Casual Contract Day-to-day basis Convert to permanent after continuous service (typically 3 months)
Probationary Contract Trial period (max 6 months) Shorter notice period, performance evaluation basis

3. Legal Grounds for Termination

The Employment Act recognizes several valid grounds for termination:

  • Misconduct: Serious breach of employment terms or company policies
  • Poor Performance: Consistent failure to meet job requirements after warnings
  • Redundancy: Genuine business restructuring or economic reasons
  • Incapacity: Physical or mental inability to perform duties
  • Mutual Agreement: Both parties agree to terminate the relationship
  • Contract Expiry: For fixed-term contracts only

4. What Constitutes Unfair Termination?

Termination may be deemed unfair if:

Ground 1

Procedural Unfairness

Failure to follow fair procedure, natural justice, or disciplinary processes.

Ground 2

Substantive Unfairness

No valid reason for termination or reason not related to employment.

Ground 3

Discriminatory Reasons

Termination based on race, gender, religion, disability, or other protected characteristics.

Ground 4

Retaliatory Actions

Termination for exercising legal rights (whistleblowing, union activities, etc.).

5. Lessons from Recent Kenyan Court Decisions

The Employment and Labour Relations Court (ELRC) has developed a rich body of case law that shapes how termination disputes are decided. These decisions reinforce that a valid reason alone is not enough; a fair process must also be followed.

"An employer who terminates the services of an employee must prove both that there was a valid and fair reason for the termination and that a fair procedure was followed. The two requirements are cumulative, not alternative."

— Walter Ogal Anuro v. Teachers Service Commission [2013] eKLR

This principle has been consistently applied and expanded in later decisions. In Kenya Airways Limited v. Aviation & Allied Workers Union Kenya & 3 Others [2014] eKLR, the Supreme Court underscored that even in genuine redundancy, employers must consult affected employees, apply fair selection criteria, and issue proper notice to both the employees and the labour office.

Case Reference Key Issue Court's Finding
Walter Ogal Anuro v. TSC [2013] eKLR Meaning of fair termination Both substantive justification and fair procedure are mandatory
Kenya Airways v. AAWUK [2014] eKLR Redundancy procedure Consultation, fair selection and statutory notice required
Postal Corporation of Kenya v. Andrew K. Tanui [2019] eKLR Right to be heard before dismissal Failure to grant a fair hearing rendered dismissal unfair
CMC Aviation v. Mohammed Noor [2015] eKLR Burden of proof Employer bears the burden of proving the reason for termination

The common thread across these authorities is procedural fairness: employees must be informed of the allegations against them, be given an opportunity to respond, and be allowed representation during disciplinary hearings under Section 41 of the Employment Act.

6. Remedies for Unfair Termination

Employees can seek various remedies through the Employment and Labour Relations Court:

  • Reinstatement: Return to former position with back pay
  • Compensation: Up to 12 months' gross salary
  • Severance Pay: For redundancy cases (15 days pay per year of service)
  • Notice Pay: Payment in lieu of proper notice period
  • Certificate of Service: Mandatory document upon termination
  • Accrued Benefits: Payment of all outstanding salaries and benefits
