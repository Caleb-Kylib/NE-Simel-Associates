Kenya's Artificial Intelligence Bill 2026: What You Need to Know

9 min read May 29, 2026
Reader note: This article provides general information, not legal advice for a specific matter. Laws and procedures can change; confirm current requirements and seek advice on your circumstances. Please verify the cited legislation and decisions before relying on them.

Artificial intelligence is no longer a distant concept in Kenya. From credit-scoring algorithms and chatbots to automated recruitment tools, AI systems now touch the daily lives of millions. Kenya's proposed Artificial Intelligence Bill 2026 seeks to create the country's first dedicated framework for governing how these systems are built, deployed and monitored. This article breaks down what the Bill aims to achieve and what it means for you.

1. Why Kenya Needs AI Legislation

Kenya has positioned itself as a regional technology hub, often called the "Silicon Savannah". Yet until now, AI has largely operated in a legal grey area, governed only indirectly through the Data Protection Act, 2019 and the Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act, 2018. The AI Bill 2026 responds to growing concerns about algorithmic bias, opaque automated decisions, and the misuse of personal data in training AI models.

The Bill is also aligned with Kenya's National AI Strategy 2025–2030, which envisions responsible AI adoption that supports economic growth while protecting fundamental rights guaranteed under the Constitution of Kenya, 2010.

2. Key Provisions of the Bill

While the Bill continues to move through public participation and parliamentary stages, its core provisions address the following areas:

  • Risk-Based Classification: AI systems are categorised by risk level, with stricter obligations for high-risk applications such as those used in healthcare, finance, and law enforcement.
  • Transparency Requirements: Individuals must be informed when they are interacting with an AI system or subject to an automated decision.
  • Human Oversight: High-risk systems must allow for meaningful human review of automated outcomes.
  • Accountability: Developers and deployers must maintain documentation and conduct impact assessments.
  • Data Governance: Training data must be lawfully obtained and managed in line with the Data Protection Act, 2019.

4. How the Bill Connects to Existing Kenyan Law

The AI Bill does not operate in isolation. It builds on established protections, particularly around data and privacy. The right to privacy under Article 31 of the Constitution has already been tested in the courts. In Katiba Institute v. Data Protection Commissioner & Another [2023] eKLR, the High Court reinforced that automated systems processing personal data must comply with data protection principles, a theme central to the new Bill.

Automated processing of personal information without a lawful basis and adequate safeguards offends the constitutional right to privacy and the Data Protection Act.

5. What Businesses Should Do Now

Organisations deploying AI should begin preparing before the Bill becomes law:

  • Audit your AI systems: Identify where automated decision-making occurs and classify the associated risk.
  • Review data sources: Ensure training and input data is lawfully collected and processed.
  • Document decisions: Maintain records of how models are built, tested and monitored.
  • Build in human oversight: Create clear escalation paths for contested automated outcomes.
  • Update privacy notices: Disclose the use of AI to customers and employees.
