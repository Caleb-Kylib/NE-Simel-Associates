What Happens to Your Property When You Die Without a Will in Kenya?

7 min read January 22, 2026

When a person dies without a valid will in Kenya, their estate is distributed according to the Law of Succession Act. This process, known as intestate succession, follows a rigid statutory formula that may not reflect the deceased's wishes or family circumstances.

Who Inherits Under Intestate Succession?

The Law of Succession Act establishes a clear order of inheritance based on family relationships:

1. Surviving Spouse and Children

If you leave a spouse and children:

  • Personal and household effects: Go entirely to the spouse
  • Remaining estate: Divided as follows:
    • Spouse receives a life interest in the whole estate
    • Upon spouse's death, the estate passes to the children

2. Surviving Spouse Only (No Children)

If you leave a spouse but no children:

  • The spouse inherits the entire estate absolutely
  • If there are surviving parents, they may have a claim to certain properties

3. Children Only (No Surviving Spouse)

If you leave children but no spouse:

  • Children inherit the estate in equal shares
  • If any child has died leaving children, those grandchildren inherit their parent's share

4. Parents, Siblings, and Extended Family

If you leave no spouse or children, the estate passes to:

  • Parents in equal shares
  • If no parents, to brothers and sisters in equal shares
  • If no siblings, to half-brothers and half-sisters
  • If no half-siblings, to grandparents
  • If no grandparents, to uncles and aunts

The Role of the Public Trustee

When there are no identifiable heirs, or when minor children are involved, the Public Trustee may administer the estate. This government office:

  • Manages estates with no known beneficiaries
  • Acts as guardian for minor children's inheritances
  • Distributes assets according to the statutory formula
  • Charges administrative fees for these services

Common Problems with Intestate Succession

Dying without a will often leads to:

  • Family disputes: Multiple claims on the same property
  • Delayed distribution: Lengthy court processes
  • Unexpected heirs: Distant relatives you may not have intended to benefit
  • Excluded dependents: Individuals you supported but who aren't statutory heirs
  • Administrative burdens: Complex legal procedures for family members
