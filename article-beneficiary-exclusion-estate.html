Can a Beneficiary Be Excluded From an Estate?

6 min read March 26, 2026

While testators in Kenya generally enjoy testamentary freedom to distribute their property as they wish, certain legal limitations protect dependents from being completely excluded from inheritance. Understanding these boundaries is crucial for effective estate planning.

1. Testamentary Freedom vs. Legal Limitations

Kenyan law balances two important principles:

Testamentary Freedom Legal Limitations
Right to decide who inherits your property Duty to provide for dependents
Can choose beneficiaries of your choice Cannot completely disinherit certain dependents
Can give unequal shares to children Must consider dependents' needs and circumstances
Can exclude distant relatives Spouses and children have special protection

2. Protected Dependents Under Kenyan Law

The Law of Succession Act protects these categories of dependents:

  • Spouse(s): Including widow, widower, or surviving spouses in polygamous marriages
  • Children: Including minor children, adult disabled children, and adopted children
  • Parents: If they were dependent on the deceased
  • Siblings: If they were dependent on the deceased
  • Other Relatives: Who were being maintained by the deceased

3. Grounds for Exclusion That May Be Challenged

Excluding a dependent may be challenged if:

  • The exclusion leaves the dependent without reasonable provision
  • The dependent was financially reliant on the deceased
  • The exclusion appears vindictive or unreasonable
  • There's evidence of undue influence or lack of capacity
  • The will was made without knowledge of the dependent's existence

4. Application for Reasonable Provision

Excluded dependents can apply to court for reasonable provision under Section 26 of the Law of Succession Act. The court considers:

Factor 1

Financial Needs

The applicant's financial resources, earning capacity, and needs.

Factor 2

Relationship Nature

The nature of relationship between applicant and deceased.

Factor 3

Other Obligations

The deceased's obligations and responsibilities to others.

Factor 4

Estate Size

The size and nature of the deceased's estate.

Factor 5

Conduct

The applicant's conduct toward the deceased.

5. Strategic Estate Planning Considerations

To minimize disputes when excluding beneficiaries:

  • Document Reasons: Consider including explanation in will or separate letter
  • Provide Nominal Gifts: Leaving a small token can reduce grounds for challenge
  • Consider Trusts: Provide for excluded dependents through discretionary trusts
  • Seek Legal Advice: Professional drafting can anticipate and address challenges
  • Update Regularly: Review estate plans as family circumstances change
