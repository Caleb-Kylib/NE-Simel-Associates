How to Transfer Property After the Death of a Loved One

7 min read March 12, 2026

Transferring property after someone's death involves navigating Kenyan succession law, court procedures, and land registry requirements. This guide walks you through the essential steps for a smooth and legally compliant property transfer.

1. Obtain Succession Documents

Before any property transfer, you need legal authority from the court:

  • Grant of Probate: If there's a valid will (executor obtains this)
  • Letters of Administration: If no will exists (administrator obtains this)
  • Confirmation of Grant: Court order confirming distribution to specific beneficiaries
  • Death Certificate: Certified copy from Registrar of Births and Deaths

2. Prepare Required Documentation

Gather these documents for the property transfer:

  • Original title deed or copy from lands registry
  • Certificate of official search (showing current ownership)
  • Death certificate of deceased owner
  • Grant of Probate or Letters of Administration
  • Confirmation of Grant showing property distribution
  • Identity documents of beneficiaries
  • Passport photos of beneficiaries
  • Land rates clearance certificate

3. Land Registry Procedures

Follow these steps at the relevant lands registry:

Step 1

Conduct Official Search

Verify property ownership, encumbrances, and restrictions at lands registry.

Step 2

Prepare Transfer Documents

Complete transfer forms (Land Form 14RL, 19RL) and have them executed.

Step 3

Pay Stamp Duty

Calculate and pay applicable stamp duty (often exempt for succession transfers).

Step 4

Submit for Registration

Submit all documents to lands registry for registration of new owners.

Step 5

Collect New Title

Receive new title deed(s) in beneficiaries' names after processing.

4. Special Considerations

Be aware of these important factors:

  • Jointly Owned Property: Survivorship rules may apply
  • Matrimonial Property: Spousal rights under Matrimonial Property Act
  • Agricultural Land: Succession restrictions may apply
  • Charged Property: Bank/mortgage company consent needed
  • Company-Owned Property: Different transfer procedures apply

5. Common Challenges and Solutions

Challenge Solution
Missing Title Deed Apply for replacement through lands registry with court order
Family Disputes Mediation or court determination of shares before transfer
Outstanding Land Rates Clear all arrears before transfer can proceed
Multiple Beneficiaries Transfer to all names or subdivide if desired
Unregistered Property First register property, then transfer through succession
