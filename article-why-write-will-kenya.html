Why You Should Write a Will in Kenya

8 min read January 15, 2026

A will is more than just a legal document—it's a declaration of your final wishes and a tool to protect your family's future. In Kenya, where family property and succession matters can become complex, having a valid will is essential for ensuring your assets are distributed according to your intentions.

1. Control Over Your Estate Distribution

Without a will, your property will be distributed according to the Law of Succession Act, which follows a fixed formula. This may not reflect your personal wishes or family circumstances. A will allows you to:

  • Specify exactly who inherits what property
  • Allocate specific assets to specific beneficiaries
  • Provide for minor children's education and welfare
  • Designate guardians for your children
  • Make provisions for extended family or charitable causes

2. Avoid Family Conflicts and Disputes

Intestate succession (dying without a will) often leads to family conflicts as relatives dispute over property distribution. A clear, legally valid will:

  • Reduces ambiguity about your intentions
  • Minimizes grounds for legal challenges
  • Provides clear instructions for asset distribution
  • Helps prevent expensive and lengthy court battles

3. Expedite the Succession Process

The probate process for estates with valid wills is generally faster than intestate succession proceedings. With a will:

  • The court can issue a grant of probate more quickly
  • Your appointed executor can begin administration immediately
  • Beneficiaries receive their inheritance sooner
  • Administrative costs are often lower

4. Protect Vulnerable Family Members

A will allows you to make special provisions for:

  • Minor children: Set up trusts or designate guardians
  • Disabled dependents: Ensure continued care and support
  • Elderly parents: Provide for their ongoing needs
  • Spouses in polygamous marriages: Clarify distribution among multiple households

5. Tax Planning and Asset Protection

Proper estate planning through a will can help:

  • Minimize estate taxes and other liabilities
  • Protect assets from creditors' claims
  • Preserve family wealth for future generations
  • Structure bequests to reduce administrative burdens
