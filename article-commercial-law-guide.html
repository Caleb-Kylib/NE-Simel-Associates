Essential Commercial Law Guide for Kenyan Businesses

10 min read June 19, 2026

Starting and running a business in Kenya offers immense opportunity, but it also carries significant legal responsibilities. From the moment you choose a business structure to the day-to-day management of contracts and compliance, sound legal foundations protect your enterprise from costly disputes. This guide walks through the essential commercial law considerations for Kenyan businesses.

1. Choosing the Right Business Structure

The Companies Act, 2015 and the Business Registration Service govern how businesses are formed in Kenya. Choosing the correct structure affects your liability, tax obligations, and ability to raise capital.

Structure Liability Best Suited For
Sole Proprietorship Unlimited personal liability Small, single-owner ventures
Partnership Shared, often unlimited Professional firms and joint ventures
Limited Liability Partnership Limited to contribution Professional partnerships seeking protection
Private Limited Company Limited to shareholding Most growing businesses

2. The Company as a Separate Legal Person

A cornerstone of company law is that a registered company is a separate legal entity from its owners. This principle, established in the classic authority Salomon v. Salomon & Co Ltd [1897] AC 22 and consistently applied by Kenyan courts, means the company can own property, sue, and be sued in its own name, and shareholders enjoy limited liability.

A company is at law a different person altogether from its subscribers; its debts and obligations are its own and not those of the individual members.

— Principle of separate legal personality, applied throughout Kenyan company law

However, Kenyan courts will "lift the corporate veil" where the company structure is used to commit fraud or evade legal obligations, holding the individuals behind it personally accountable.

3. Contracts: The Backbone of Commerce

Every business relationship rests on contracts. To be enforceable in Kenya, a contract generally requires offer, acceptance, consideration, intention to create legal relations, and capacity. Well-drafted contracts should address:

  • Clear obligations: Precise description of each party's duties.
  • Payment terms: Amounts, timelines and consequences of default.
  • Dispute resolution: Mediation or arbitration clauses to avoid costly litigation.
  • Termination: Grounds and notice periods for ending the relationship.
  • Governing law: Confirmation that Kenyan law applies.

4. Protecting Intellectual Property

Intellectual property is often a business's most valuable asset. Kenya protects IP through the Kenya Industrial Property Institute (KIPI) and the Copyright Act. Consider:

  • Trademarks: Register your brand name and logo to prevent imitation.
  • Patents: Protect novel inventions and processes.
  • Copyright: Safeguard original creative and software works.
  • Trade secrets: Use confidentiality agreements to protect know-how.

5. Regulatory Compliance

Businesses must remain compliant across multiple regulatory areas to operate lawfully:

Area 1

Tax Compliance

KRA registration, PIN, VAT, and timely filing of returns.

Area 2

Data Protection

Compliance with the Data Protection Act, 2019 when handling personal data.

Area 3

Licensing

Sector-specific and county business permits.

Area 4

Employment Law

Compliant contracts and adherence to the Employment Act.
