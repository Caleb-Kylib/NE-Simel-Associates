Defamation on Social Media: Can You Sue Someone for a Facebook, WhatsApp or Twitter Post?

8 min read June 26, 2026

A single post, tweet or WhatsApp message can spread to thousands of people within minutes and can seriously damage a person's reputation. In Kenya, the law of defamation applies just as firmly online as it does in newspapers or on television. If a false statement about you is published on social media, you may well have grounds to sue.

1. What Is Defamation?

Defamation is the publication of a false statement that lowers a person's reputation in the eyes of right-thinking members of society. It takes two forms: libel (written or permanent form, which includes most social media posts) and slander (spoken form). Because posts on Facebook, X (Twitter) and WhatsApp are written and stored, they typically fall under libel.

2. What You Must Prove

To succeed in a defamation claim in Kenya, a claimant must establish the following:

Element What It Means
Defamatory Statement The words lower your reputation or expose you to ridicule
Reference to You The statement identifies you, directly or indirectly
Publication The statement was communicated to at least one other person
Falsity & Harm The statement is untrue and has caused or is likely to cause harm

On social media, "publication" is easily satisfied, posting to a group, timeline or status means the statement has been seen by others. Even forwarding or sharing a defamatory message can amount to fresh publication.

3. Kenyan Courts Take Online Defamation Seriously

Kenyan courts have repeatedly awarded substantial damages for defamatory statements made on social media, recognising the speed and reach of these platforms.

Statements posted on social media platforms are published the moment they are accessible to others, and their wide and rapid circulation can aggravate the damage to a person's reputation.

In cases such as Cyprian Andama v. Director of Public Prosecutions & Others [2019] eKLR, the courts examined the boundaries of online speech, while numerous High Court decisions have awarded damages ranging from hundreds of thousands to several million shillings against individuals who posted false and damaging statements on Facebook and X.

4. Available Defences

Not every unpleasant post is actionable. A person sued for defamation may rely on recognised defences:

  • Justification (Truth): The statement is substantially true.
  • Fair Comment: An honest opinion on a matter of public interest.
  • Privilege: Statements made in protected settings, such as court or Parliament.
  • Freedom of Expression: Balanced against reputation under Article 33 of the Constitution.

5. What to Do If You Are Defamed Online

  • Preserve evidence: Take screenshots showing the content, author, date and URL.
  • Do not retaliate: Responding in kind may expose you to a counterclaim.
  • Send a demand letter: A formal letter may secure an apology and retraction.
  • Report to the platform: Request removal of the offending content.
  • Consult an advocate: Assess the strength of your claim and remedies available.
