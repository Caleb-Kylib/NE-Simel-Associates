Testate vs Intestate Succession in Kenya

6 min read January 29, 2026

In Kenyan succession law, how your estate is distributed depends on whether you die testate (with a valid will) or intestate (without a valid will). Understanding these two distinct legal pathways is crucial for effective estate planning.

Testate Succession: Dying With a Valid Will

Testate succession occurs when a person dies leaving a valid will. The will becomes the primary guide for estate distribution.

Key Characteristics:

  • Executor appointed: The will names an executor to administer the estate
  • Grant of probate: Court confirms the will's validity and authorizes the executor
  • Testator's wishes: Estate distributed according to the deceased's expressed intentions
  • Specific bequests: Can designate specific assets to specific beneficiaries
  • Guardianship provisions: Can appoint guardians for minor children

Advantages of Testate Succession:

  • Control: You decide exactly who gets what
  • Clarity: Reduces ambiguity and potential disputes
  • Efficiency: Generally faster probate process
  • Flexibility: Can make complex arrangements for family needs
  • Tax planning: Can structure bequests to minimize tax liabilities

Intestate Succession: Dying Without a Valid Will

Intestate succession occurs when a person dies without a valid will, or when the will is declared invalid.

Key Characteristics:

  • Statutory formula: Estate distributed according to Law of Succession Act
  • Letters of administration: Court appoints an administrator
  • Fixed hierarchy: Strict order of inheritance based on family relationships
  • No special provisions: Cannot make specific arrangements for special needs
  • Public Trustee involvement: May administer estates with no clear beneficiaries

Disadvantages of Intestate Succession:

  • Loss of control: Your wishes are not considered
  • Potential disputes: Family conflicts over statutory distributions
  • Delays: Often takes longer than testate succession
  • Unintended beneficiaries: Distant relatives may inherit
  • No special provisions: Cannot provide for non-family dependents

Comparative Table: Testate vs Intestate Succession

Aspect Testate Succession Intestate Succession
Legal Basis Deceased's will Law of Succession Act
Estate Administrator Executor named in will Administrator appointed by court
Court Grant Grant of probate Letters of administration
Distribution Control Complete control by testator Fixed statutory formula
Time to Completion Generally 6-12 months Often 12-24 months or more
Costs Lower, more predictable Higher, less predictable

Making the Right Choice for Your Family

The choice between testate and intestate succession is really a choice between planning and leaving things to chance. For most Kenyans, especially those with:

  • Significant assets or property
  • Minor children
  • Complex family situations (multiple marriages, dependents with special needs)
  • Specific charitable intentions
  • Business interests to transfer

...creating a valid will is the only way to ensure your estate is distributed according to your wishes.
