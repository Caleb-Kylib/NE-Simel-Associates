Who Can Inherit Your Property Under Kenyan Law?

7 min read February 12, 2026

Kenyan succession law establishes clear rules about who can inherit property, whether you die with a will (testate) or without one (intestate). Understanding these rules is crucial for effective estate planning.

1. Testamentary Freedom and Its Limits

In Kenya, you have considerable freedom to decide who inherits your property through a will. However, this freedom has important limitations:

  • Dependents' rights: You cannot completely disinherit dependents who were maintained by you
  • Public policy: Provisions that violate public policy or morality may be invalid
  • Forced heirship: Certain categories of heirs have statutory protection

2. Statutory Heirs Under Intestate Succession

If you die without a will, the Law of Succession Act determines who inherits in this order:

A. Class I Heirs: Spouse and Children

  • Surviving spouse: Receives personal and household effects plus a life interest
  • Children: Inherit the remainder after the spouse's life interest ends
  • Equal division: Children share equally regardless of age or gender

B. Class II Heirs: Parents

If no spouse or children, the estate goes to:

  • Parents in equal shares
  • If only one parent survives, that parent takes all

C. Class III Heirs: Siblings

If no Class I or II heirs, the estate passes to:

  • Brothers and sisters in equal shares
  • Includes half-brothers and half-sisters

D. Class IV Heirs: Other Relatives

If no closer relatives, the estate goes to:

  • Grandparents
  • Uncles and aunts
  • Cousins and other relatives

3. Special Categories of Beneficiaries

A. Dependents

Even if not mentioned in a will, dependents who were maintained by you may apply to court for reasonable provision from your estate.

B. Illegitimate Children

Under Kenyan law, all children have equal inheritance rights regardless of parents' marital status.

C. Adopted Children

Legally adopted children have the same inheritance rights as biological children.

D. Polygamous Families

In polygamous marriages, each spouse and their children have inheritance rights according to statutory formulas.

4. Property That Cannot Be Inherited

Certain types of property have special rules:

  • Customary land: May follow traditional inheritance rules
  • Joint tenancy property: Passes automatically to surviving joint tenant
  • Life insurance proceeds: Go directly to named beneficiaries
  • Trust property: Distributed according to trust terms
  • Company shares: Subject to company articles of association

5. Practical Considerations for Estate Planning

When planning your estate, consider:

  • Blended families: Children from previous relationships
  • Special needs dependents: Long-term care requirements
  • Business interests: Continuity and management succession
  • International assets: Different countries have different inheritance laws
  • Tax implications: Inheritance tax and other liabilities
