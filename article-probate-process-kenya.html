The Probate Process in Kenya Explained

8 min read February 26, 2026

Probate is the legal process through which a deceased person's will is validated and their estate is administered according to Kenyan law. Understanding this process is crucial for executors, beneficiaries, and anyone involved in estate management.

1. Filing the Probate Application

The probate process begins when the executor named in the will files a probate application at the High Court. The required documents include:

  • The original will (or a certified copy if original is lost)
  • Death certificate of the deceased
  • Completed probate application forms (P&A 1, P&A 4)
  • Affidavit of executor(s) confirming their willingness to serve
  • List of assets and liabilities of the deceased
  • Proposed sureties for the executor(s)

2. Publication of Notice

After filing, a notice is published in the Kenya Gazette and a local newspaper inviting objections. This 30-day period allows:

  • Creditors to file claims against the estate
  • Family members or beneficiaries to raise objections
  • Anyone with interest to challenge the will's validity

3. Grant of Probate Issuance

If no objections are raised, the court issues a Grant of Probate to the executor(s). This document:

  • Formally recognizes the will as valid
  • Authorizes the executor(s) to administer the estate
  • Gives legal authority to collect assets, pay debts, and distribute property

4. Estate Administration Duties

As executors, their responsibilities include:

  • Collecting and safeguarding all estate assets
  • Paying valid debts, taxes, and funeral expenses
  • Preparing an inventory of assets and liabilities
  • Managing estate assets until final distribution
  • Keeping accurate accounts of all transactions

5. Final Distribution and Discharge

Once all debts are paid and taxes settled, the executor distributes the remaining assets to beneficiaries. This involves:

  • Transferring property titles to beneficiaries
  • Closing bank accounts and distributing funds
  • Filing final accounts with the court
  • Applying for discharge after completing all duties
